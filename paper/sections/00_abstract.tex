\begin{abstract}
Large language models (LLMs) can post-edit neural machine translation (NMT) drafts, but an editor that repairs errors can also change what a draft already rendered correctly. We evaluate this trade-off for English--Tagalog and English--Spanish in both directions on the held-out FLORES+ devtest set (1{,}012 sentences per direction). A sentence-split NLLB-200 3.3B system supplies the draft; Llama 3 8B Instruct and GPT-4o Mini edit it zero-shot, with one retrieved development exemplar, or with a single-call reason-then-edit format. Prompts, output contracts, and analyses were frozen on development data before the test set was opened, and all 24{,}288 outputs are evaluated. GPT-4o Mini improves COMET-22 in all twelve conditions (+0.26 to +1.00 points, all significant after Holm correction), most for the Tagalog pair and with the retrieved exemplar. Llama 3 8B never improves significantly; it is significantly worse in six conditions, and its reason-then-edit outputs degrade 247 of 1{,}179 strong drafts (20.9\%), against at most 4.3\% for any GPT-4o Mini strategy. Editing helps weak drafts almost everywhere, whereas strong drafts are at best preserved. Average quality, preservation of strong drafts, and output validity should therefore be assessed separately before an LLM post-editor is deployed.
\end{abstract}
