\section{Related Work and Contribution Boundary}
\subsection{LLM post-editing and diagnostic feedback}
Raunak et al.~\cite{raunak2023} evaluate GPT-4 as a direct translation post-editor and report both quality improvements and hallucinated edits. Ki and Carpuat~\cite{ki2024} investigate external error annotations for LLM post-editing, including prompting and fine-tuning. Their findings motivate treating the usefulness of feedback as an empirical question. Our diagnostic condition instead asks an unmodified editor to produce its own short error categories. Those self-generated categories are observable outputs, not human annotations or verified explanations.

xTower~\cite{treviso2024} explicitly studies explanations and corrected translations, including expert assessment of explanation quality. Our experiment does not reproduce that trained system or its explanation evaluation. It asks whether a short diagnostic contract is operationally reliable for two fixed editors. LangMark~\cite{velazquez2025} provides multilingual source--MT--human-post-edit triplets, including Spanish. FLORES+ instead supplies translation references, not human minimal post-edits. Our reference-based gains therefore measure translation quality proxies and cannot establish reduced human editing effort.

\subsection{Examples and multilingual representations}
Vilar et al.~\cite{vilar2023} investigate translation prompting and example selection. We use LaBSE~\cite{feng2022} to rank development sources by cosine similarity and supply one parallel demonstration. Similarity is semantic: the retrieval procedure does not independently measure syntactic similarity. A single deterministic exemplar makes the comparison reproducible, but does not establish that one exemplar or this encoder is optimal.

\subsection{Relationship to the earlier ICAI study}
The supplied ICAI manuscript~\cite{ahmadnia2026icai} studies English-to-German domain adaptation with a frozen OPUS-MT draft, GPT-4 Turbo, multilingual Sentence-BERT top-three exemplars, and an optional second terminology-verification call. Frozen-NMT anchoring and dense exemplar retrieval are substantive shared methods. We cite these components as established, and do not claim the first hybrid NMT--LLM pipeline or first retrieval-based post-editor.

Norris changes the empirical setting: a stronger NLLB baseline, four Tagalog/Spanish directions, commercial and open-weight editors, and explicit analysis of strict output-contract failure and over-editing. Our reason-then-edit uses one generation call, rather than ICAI's second verification call. Different data, models, metrics, and controls preclude numerical cross-paper superiority claims. The exact publication metadata of the supplied ICAI version must be reconciled before submission; its title and authors are verified from the supplied paper.

\subsection{Evaluation}
BLEU~\cite{papineni2002}, chrF/chrF++~\cite{popovic2015,popovic2017}, and source-aware COMET~\cite{rei2020,rei2022} offer complementary views. Reproducible lexical scoring requires full metric signatures~\cite{post2018}. Neither lexical overlap nor a neural scalar replaces source inspection. Our evaluation therefore reports measured quality changes alongside preservation screens and contract failures, without treating any screen as a gold error annotation.
