\section{Introduction}
An automatic post-editor must decide both what to correct and what to leave alone. Large language models (LLMs) can repair neural machine translation (NMT) output~\cite{raunak2023,ki2024,treviso2024}, but a fluent rewrite can also alter a name, number, or source relation that the draft already expressed correctly, and LLM editors are known to produce hallucinated edits~\cite{raunak2023}.

\textbf{Problem.} When drafts come from a strong multilingual NMT system, most sentences need little or no change. An editor can then raise the average score while damaging many correct drafts, and a mean difference cannot reveal this trade-off. For a lower-resource pair such as English--Tagalog, evidence is limited on whether an LLM editor adds value or risk on top of a strong baseline, and on whether the answer depends on the editor, the prompting strategy, or the translation direction. In deployment, an editor that silently degrades correct output is worse than none.

\textbf{Approach.} This study evaluates the trade-off for English--Tagalog and English--Spanish in both directions. A fixed NLLB-200 3.3B system~\cite{nllb2022} produces sentence-split drafts, and two instruction-following editors, open-weight Llama 3 8B Instruct and commercial GPT-4o Mini, receive the source and the draft. The two language pairs provide a limited resource contrast; language, direction, domain, and baseline quality remain intertwined, so pair and direction differences are reported descriptively rather than as causal resource effects. The study addresses four questions:
\textbf{RQ1:} How do editing gains and harms differ between the Tagalog and Spanish pairs, conditional on baseline quality?
\textbf{RQ2:} How do the three prompting strategies compare within each model and direction?
\textbf{RQ3:} When do editors change already strong drafts and degrade their measured quality or source fidelity?
\textbf{RQ4:} How do effects differ between the two directions of a language pair?

This is an empirical evaluation, not a new post-editing architecture. Anchoring an LLM editor on a frozen NMT draft and selecting in-context exemplars by semantic similarity follow established prior work~\cite{ahmadnia2026icai,vilar2023}, and we do not claim either as a contribution. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item a held-out evaluation in a new empirical setting (four Tagalog/Spanish directions) against a stronger, sentence-split NLLB-200 baseline, with the protocol frozen on development data before the test partition was opened;
\item a two-editor comparison (open-weight and commercial) across three prompting strategies, with paired confirmatory tests and Holm multiplicity control;
\item explicit failure accounting: every planned output is retained, and output-contract failures are reported as outcomes rather than discarded;
\item an over-editing and preservation analysis of strong drafts that separates metric-defined harm from heuristic preservation screens.
\end{itemize}
The central finding is that post-editing helps only when the editor leaves adequate drafts alone; neither edit frequency nor explicit reasoning explains the difference between the editors.
