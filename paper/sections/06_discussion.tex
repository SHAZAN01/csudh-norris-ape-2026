\section{Discussion}
\textbf{What separates the editors is not how often they edit.} Both editors change most drafts under one-shot prompting, but GPT-4o Mini's changes to strong drafts are rarely harmful, whereas Llama's frequently are. Its losses concentrate where S0 is already good: in the protect pool, Llama's mean change ranges from $-0.33$ to $-2.09$ points across conditions, while its improve-pool changes are positive in 11 of 12 conditions. Even an oracle gate that left strong drafts untouched (it needs the reference, so it is not deployable) would keep Llama below S0 in 6 of 12 conditions, because it also degrades mid-quality drafts. A post-editor is therefore only useful if it leaves correct drafts alone, and protecting strong drafts is necessary but not sufficient.

\textbf{Explicit reasoning did not help.} Reason-then-edit was never significantly better than zero-shot or one-shot, its point estimate was lower in all 16 such contrasts, and for Llama it was the most harmful strategy in every direction. The development pilot showed why this must be measured with a valid contract: under the original strict contract, 227 of 400 Llama reason-then-edit outputs fell back to the baseline, hiding the over-editing that the revised, fully valid condition exposes on held-out data. Diagnoses are model outputs, not verified explanations.

\textbf{Source review of held-out cases.} We read 96 held-out cases selected by fixed rules before any text was inspected (per condition: the largest strong-draft over-edit, the largest gain, and the first digit-loss and language-ID flags). All 12 of the largest Llama over-edits were genuine errors, for example rendering a plane's \textit{secuestro} as ``kidnapping'' rather than ``hijacking'' under all three strategies, or turning ``least interesting'' into ``not unwilling''. In contrast, 10 of the 12 largest GPT-4o Mini over-edits were acceptable or even source-supported changes that COMET penalized, such as restoring ``luxury'' where S0 had ``glory'' ($-9.1$ points). The largest gains repaired clear S0 errors, such as repetition loops, though some repairs remained partly wrong. Llama reason-then-edit sometimes returned the untranslated source while its diagnosis claimed a one-word fix; language identification places 20 of its 4{,}048 outputs in the source language. Most digit and language flags reflected formatting or names. These cases illustrate mechanisms and are not independent bilingual ratings.
