\section{Discussion}
\textbf{Preservation, not edit frequency, separates the editors (RQ3).} In the protect pool, Llama's mean change ranges from $-0.33$ to $-2.09$ points across conditions, while its improve-pool changes are positive in 11 of 12 conditions: it repairs weak drafts but also rewrites good ones.

\textbf{Where the gains come from (RQ1).} An exact decomposition of each mean change by pool locates gains and losses (Fig.~\ref{fig:pools}). Weak drafts make up only 6--18\% of items per direction yet supply 41--77\% of each GPT-4o Mini gain, while strong drafts contribute almost nothing. For Llama, losses on mid-quality and strong drafts outweigh its weak-draft repairs. Even an oracle gate that left strong drafts untouched (it needs the reference, so it is not deployable) would keep Llama below S0 in 6 of 12 conditions, because it also degrades mid-quality drafts. Protecting strong drafts is therefore necessary but not sufficient.

\textbf{Explicit reasoning did not help (RQ2).} Reason-then-edit was never significantly better than zero-shot or one-shot, its point estimate was lower in all 16 such contrasts, and for Llama it was the most harmful strategy in every direction. Every held-out reason-then-edit output passed the output contract, so these losses reflect editing rather than format failure. Diagnoses are model outputs, not verified explanations.

\textbf{Source- and reference-based inspection.} The inspection covered 96 held-out cases selected by fixed rules before any text was read (per condition: the largest strong-draft over-edit, the largest gain, and the first digit-loss and language-ID flags). All 12 of the largest Llama over-edits were genuine errors, for example rendering a plane's \textit{secuestro} as ``kidnapping'' rather than ``hijacking'' under all three strategies, or turning ``least interesting'' into ``not unwilling''. In contrast, 10 of the 12 largest GPT-4o Mini over-edits were acceptable or even source-supported changes that COMET penalized, such as restoring ``luxury'' where S0 had ``glory'' ($-9.1$ points). The largest gains repaired clear S0 errors such as repetition loops, some only partly. Llama reason-then-edit sometimes returned the untranslated source while its diagnosis claimed a one-word fix; language identification places 20 of its 4{,}048 outputs in the source language. Most digit and language flags reflected formatting or names. This is a qualitative inspection, not a human evaluation; the cases illustrate mechanisms rather than error rates.

\textbf{Implications.} First, preservation of strong drafts should be reported alongside mean quality, since the editors differ far more in over-editing than in edit rate. Second, a small editor such as Llama 3 8B should edit only drafts that a reference-free quality estimator, tuned on development data, flags as weak; the oracle shows headroom, not an achievable gate. Third, one retrieved exemplar is an inexpensive gain for a strong editor (about US\$0.10 per 1{,}000 sentences for GPT-4o Mini at list rates), whereas a diagnosis step adds tokens without benefit. The Llama over-edits match the unfaithful edits reported for LLM post-editing~\cite{raunak2023}, and GPT-4o Mini's penalized rewordings show why reference-based scores need source inspection.
