\section{Data and Evaluation Protocol}
\subsection{Partitions and sampling}
The experiment uses revision-pinned FLORES+~\cite{nllb2022,floresplus}, the maintained continuation of FLORES-200, with 997 development and 1,012 devtest items per direction. Its references are translations rather than human post-edits of our NMT drafts. The dataset's \texttt{fil\_Latn} configuration maps to NLLB's \texttt{tgl\_Latn}; English and Spanish use \texttt{eng\_Latn} and \texttt{spa\_Latn}. Spanish is the benchmark's standard variety, with no claim about heritage, dialectal, code-switched, or Los Angeles community Spanish.

A seed-42 uniform sample of 100 aligned development IDs is reused across all four directions, three strategies, and two editors. This gives 2,400 system outcomes, not 2,400 independent sentences. Conditions are balanced in count; the sample is not stratified by baseline quality. No pilot decision uses devtest scores. Older Phase 2 devtest baseline files are separate and unscored in this development workflow.

\subsection{Metrics and uncertainty}
The primary metric is reference-based \texttt{Unbabel/wmt22-comet-da}~\cite{cometmodel}, reported as native score multiplied by 100. This identifies the exact checkpoint, rather than assuming the full ensemble from the COMET-22 shared-task submission. Secondary metrics use SacreBLEU 2.6.0: mixed-case BLEU with 13a tokenization and exponential smoothing; chrF++ with character order 6, word order 2, and $\beta=2$.

For each condition, $d_i=C(x_i,\hat{y}_i,r_i)-C(x_i,b_i,r_i)$ uses native segment scores and the same source and reference. We report $100\bar d$ and a descriptive percentile interval from 10,000 paired bootstrap resamples, seed 42. The pilot reports no confirmatory $p$-values or multiplicity-adjusted significance claims. Separate baseline intervals are not tests of strategy superiority. Corpus BLEU and chrF++ are computed over all planned items, including fallback outputs.

\subsection{Editing, harm, and preservation}
Textual editing means $\hat y_i\ne b_i$, after the recorded outer-whitespace handling. Metric improvement is $d_i>0.02$, harm is $d_i<-0.02$, and the inclusive middle band is $[-0.02,0.02]$. A native difference of 0.02 equals two reported COMET points; the middle band is not textual identity or demonstrated equivalence.

The protect pool is fixed using original baseline scores $C_i\geq0.90$, and the improve pool uses $C_i<0.80$. Protect-pool over-editing requires a textual edit and metric harm. Its primary descriptive denominator is every protect item; the edited-protect denominator is also retained. These are metric-defined outcomes, not human judgments. Digit multisets, newly absent capitalized source tokens, target-language identification, and length ratios provide heuristic screens. The pilot used \texttt{langdetect} 1.0.9, not the fastText implementation named in the original roadmap. Legitimate localization and translation can trigger preservation flags.

\subsection{Execution and planned held-out study}
Both editors use temperature 0, top\_p 1, seed 42, and an output cap of 1,024 tokens. Llama runs in BF16 with vLLM 0.11.2 on an A100 80 GB, context limit 8,192. The GPT pilot uses four workers and four request starts per second. Exact revisions and environments appear in the appendix and manifest. Deterministic settings do not guarantee bit-identical remote service behavior.

The planned main matrix contains $1,012\times4\times2\times3=24,288$ outputs if the model/strategy matrix is retained. Before execution, the protocol must finalize retrieval eligibility, output policy, the complete contrast and multiplicity plan, and any documented deviations. The present manifest explicitly remains unfrozen. No held-out inference or confirmatory finding is claimed here.
